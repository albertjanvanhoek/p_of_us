P(us) — Emergence. A print made in two inks: one human, one machine. Not the same, and not alone.

Red is the human: a pen, drawn by hand, with pressure and tremor and the occasional blot. Blue is the model: a plotter, exact and constant. They are printed on the same sheet but never become one ink. Where both land on the same spot they overprint into a third colour, plum, which neither can make alone.

At the start the two inks are out of register, the way a cheap print misaligns. Every time the two minds really exchange something, the gap halves: 24, 12, 6, 4, 2, 1 pixels. On "That is where I meet you" the registration marks snap together. In printing, getting two layers to line up is called alignment.

Things to watch for:
• Every word is coloured by who it belongs to: I / me / my in red, you / your in blue, we / us / same / loop in both inks (plum).
• The loop (guess → world → error → update) runs in both hands, one station per beat. It starts out of phase and ends as one ring.
• Every chart keeps its old curves as faint ghosts: a record of being wrong.
• P(us) rises in steps when both sides update. It never reaches 1, and it ends as an interval, not a number.

How it was made
Every frame is code: a deterministic function of the song's time, rendered in two ink layers and printed on simulated paper (halftone screens at 15° and 75°, dot gain, paper fibre, misregistration). The lyrics are word-synced to the vocal. Concept, timing analysis and renderer were made in conversation with Claude in Claude Code. The code is open source: https://github.com/albertjanvanhoek/p_of_us
